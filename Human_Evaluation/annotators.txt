On the DXY-dialog dataset, human experts achieve an average precision of 77.52, an average recall of 87.17, an average F1-score of 82.05, an accuracy of 90.67, a Csdm-Score of 15.33, and an Sdm-Score of 85.09. On the IMCS-dialog dataset, the corresponding average values are 97.18 for precision, 72.07 for recall, 82.60 for F1-score, 92.00 for accuracy, 22.00 for Csdm-Score, and 72.88 for Sdm-Score. On the MDD-dialog dataset, human experts obtain an average precision of 81.11, an average recall of 89.73, an average F1-score of 85.03, an accuracy of 87.33, a Csdm-Score of 58.00, and an Sdm-Score of 85.62. These results show that human annotators maintain consistently strong performance across all datasets, particularly in metrics that require fine-grained symptom–disease consistency.
We compare these results with SPD to provide an initial indication of the model’s practical applicability. Although SPD achieves competitive accuracy, there remains a clear performance gap compared with human experts in fine-grained metrics such as Csdm-Score and Sdm-Score. This supports our conclusion that RMS-MDD is a challenging real-world benchmark and that current models, including SPD, do not yet reach clinician-level reliability.
